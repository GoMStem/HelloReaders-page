# Customized Reading Style Analysis and Recommendations for Improving Middle School English Reading Skills

To enhance English reading skills, it is not enough to simply read many books. Each student has a unique reading style, and understanding this style is crucial for receiving personalized recommendations. In this article, we will explore how middle school students can analyze their reading styles and find suitable books accordingly.

## 1. Understand Your Unique Reading Style

Students can be classified into four major reading styles: information-oriented, story-oriented, emotion-oriented, and analysis-oriented. Let’s look at each style:

- **Information-oriented reading**: Students who prioritize facts and information. They prefer gathering data, statistics, and organized information.
- **Story-oriented reading**: Students who value engaging stories and character development. They immerse themselves in the plot and emotional journey of the characters.
- **Emotion-oriented reading**: Readers who focus on emotions and experiences, finding lessons and values from the story.
- **Analysis-oriented reading**: Students who enjoy analyzing text and critiquing literature, exploring themes, symbols, and literary techniques in depth.

Students may possess one or a combination of these styles. Identifying which style resonates with them is the first step. To do this, they should reflect on what aspects of their reading captivate them and what books have left a lasting impression.

## 2. Book Recommendations Based on Reading Styles

After understanding their reading styles, it’s important to select books that match these preferences. Here are recommendations for each style:

- **Information-oriented reading**: Non-fiction books related to science, history, or social sciences are ideal. These books provide diverse perspectives and insights. For example, books like 'A Brief History of Time' by Stephen Hawking offer knowledge and understanding.
- **Story-oriented reading**: Finding engaging novels that allow immersion is key. Classic literature or contemporary YA (Young Adult) novels could be fitting. For instance, 'Harry Potter' series or 'The Hunger Games' series are great recommendations.
- **Emotion-oriented reading**: Novels that delve into human psychology and emotions suit this style. Growth narratives or family stories can offer profound insights. A good example could be 'Almond' by Won-pyung So.
- **Analysis-oriented reading**: Reading texts that incorporate literary theory and criticism can be particularly enriching. Engaging with classic novels such as 'The Great Gatsby' and analyzing them critically is beneficial.

## 3. Writing Book Reviews

Writing personal reviews of the books they read is immensely helpful for solidifying reading styles and improving reading skills. Reviews allow students to organize their thoughts systematically and provide guidance for selecting subsequent books. It is advisable for parents to encourage their children to write reviews regularly.

## 4. Participating in Reading Groups

Creating reading groups with friends who have similar styles can foster shared recommendations and discussions. Engaging in meaningful conversations adds depth to reading experiences. Additionally, exposure to various opinions helps students gain new perspectives.
